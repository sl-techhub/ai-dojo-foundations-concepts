    Three different things, easy to confuse

    The baker, the oven, the card

    The baker

    The model

    Knows how to bake in general. Give it flour, sugar and eggs and you get a cake — a good one, and a slightly different one every time.

    The oven

    A tool

    Access it does not have on its own. Without the oven nothing bakes, however good the recipe — that is a set of keys.

    The card

    A skill

    What to do with the other two, our way: the order of the steps, the proportions, and the note that says do not open the oven for the first 25 minutes.

    A fourth thing exists and is almost never what you need: teaching a whole new baker from scratch. That is fine-tuning — months, money, and a baker who still cannot be corrected by editing one line.

    The same idea, minus the cake

    The orderThe cardWhat the card holds
    “Write up the month for the board.” Monthly report Section order, the four numbers we always show, the tone, the chart template
    “Check this before it ships.” Brand check Words we do not use, claims that legal has to see, how the products are named
    “File this bug.” Bug ticket Required fields, how to title it, who it goes to, the reproduction template
    “New client starts Monday.” Client kickoff Order of the calls, the document sent on day one, what to ask before quoting

    None of these teach the model anything it could not already do. They stop you re-explaining the house rules every Monday.

    The catch. A card is a document, and it rots like one. Three ways skills go quietly wrong: the tab is too vague, so the card never comes out; the card has grown into a whole cookbook, so the one line that mattered is buried; or the card is a year old and the oven has since been replaced. Written down once is the promise — kept true is the work.
