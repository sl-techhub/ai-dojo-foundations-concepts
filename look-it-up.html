Why it finds things you did not name

Near in meaning, not in wording

Every passage is filed by what it is about, so related ones end up neighbours. Your question is placed on the same map — and whatever it lands next to is what gets pulled.

Closeness here is plain distance on a flat picture. A real system spreads the same idea over hundreds of dimensions — same principle, no way to draw it.

The looking-up, in the open

Three pages go on the desk

It finds the few passages that answer you, puts those on the desk, and writes from them.

Everything else stayed in the drawer. Here all three came from one folder; often they come from several.

What it fixes

It stops guessing. Instead of improvising from training data, it quotes your page.

What still breaks

If the search misses, the answer is built on the wrong pages, just as confidently.

What you can do

Ask in the words your documents use. Check the citation, not just the answer.

For the record. The technical name is RAG — Retrieval-Augmented Generation: the looking-up, the passages added to what it can see, the answer written from them.
